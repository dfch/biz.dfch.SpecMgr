 1.


    I want to review systems and software architectures. I want to examine these architectures in regards to common viewpoints (as outlined in ISO 42010). List common viewpoints? Does it make sense to also choose viewpoints based on ISO 25010 characteristics?

 2.


    Yes. In fact, I would recommend combining *ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010
    viewpoints with ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics* when
    reviewing software and system architectures.

    They complement each other:

      * *ISO 42010* helps you structure an architecture review around
        stakeholders, their concerns, and the architectural views needed
        to examine those concerns.
      * *ISO 25010* provides a systematic catalogue of quality
        characteristics against which you can evaluate the architecture.

    The combination gives you a much more comprehensive and repeatable
    architecture review approach.

    Let's look at both.


      1. Common architectural viewpoints (ISO 42010)

    First, an important distinction: ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 does *not
    prescribe a mandatory set of viewpoints*.

    The standard defines how viewpoints should be specified and used.
    The actual viewpoints should be selected according to the
    stakeholders and their concerns.

    The current edition is ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022. ISO

    Nevertheless, there are commonly used viewpoints in software and
    systems architecture.

    Here is a practical collection that I would recommend considering
    for architecture reviews.

    Viewpoint	What it examines	Typical concerns
    1. Context	System boundaries and its environment	Scope, external
    dependencies, interfaces
    2. Functional	System functionality and responsibilities	Functional
    completeness, decomposition, capabilities
    3. Logical	Logical components and their relationships	Modularity,
    separation of concerns, coupling
    4. Information/Data	Data structures, ownership and flows	Data
    integrity, consistency, availability
    5. Application/Software	Software components and their interactions	
    Dependencies, interfaces, reuse
    6. Technology/Infrastructure	Technology stack and infrastructure	
    Technology choices, constraints, obsolescence
    7. Deployment/Physical	Distribution of components across
    infrastructure	Deployment topology, resource allocation
    8. Integration/Interface	Communication between components and
    external systems	API design, interoperability, protocols
    9. Security	Security architecture and controls	Authentication,
    authorization, confidentiality
    10. Operational	Runtime behaviour and operational processes	
    Monitoring, logging, availability, recovery
    11. Development/Construction	Software organization and build
    processes	Maintainability, testability, extensibility
    12. Process/Behaviour	Dynamic behaviour and interactions over time	
    Concurrency, workflows, state transitions
    13. Lifecycle/Evolution	Architecture evolution over its lifecycle	
    Extensibility, migration, technical debt

    These are practical viewpoint categories, not an official list from
    ISO 42010.


          Additional viewpoints worth considering

    Depending on the system, you might also want:

      * *Business viewpoint:* Business capabilities, business processes,
        organizational responsibilities and business alignment.
      * *Performance viewpoint:* Response times, throughput, resource
        utilization and scalability.
      * *Safety viewpoint:* Hazards, safety requirements and risk
        mitigation.
      * *Compliance viewpoint:* Regulatory requirements, standards and
        auditability.
      * *Cost viewpoint:* Infrastructure costs, licensing, operating
        costs and total cost of ownership.

    Some of these can be incorporated into other viewpoints rather than
    being separate viewpoints.

    For example, security can be examined through the functional,
    deployment, data and operational views.


      2. Should you use ISO 25010 characteristics to select viewpoints?

    Yes. I would actually make this a central part of your review
    methodology.

    However, there is an important conceptual distinction:

    *ISO 25010 defines quality characteristics, not architectural
    viewpoints.*

    The characteristics describe WHAT you want to evaluate. The
    viewpoints describe HOW you examine the architecture to evaluate it.

    For example:

      * Quality characteristic: Performance efficiency.
      * Concern: Can the system handle 10,000 concurrent users?
      * Viewpoint: Deployment and infrastructure.
      * Architectural view: Deployment topology, load distribution and
        resource allocation.
      * Evaluation: Performance analysis, load testing and capacity
        assessment.

    This gives you a traceable relationship between quality requirements
    and architecture decisions.


        ISO 25010 quality characteristics

    One important detail: ISO/IEC 25010:2023 revised the quality model
    compared with the 2011 edition.

    The 2023 product quality model defines nine characteristics:

    Quality characteristic	What it addresses
    1. Functional suitability	Whether the system provides the required
    functionality
    2. Performance efficiency	Performance, resource utilization and capacity
    3. Compatibility	Coexistence and interoperability
    4. Interaction capability	Usability, accessibility and user interaction
    5. Reliability	Availability, faultlessness, recoverability and fault
    tolerance
    6. Security	Confidentiality, integrity, accountability and authenticity
    7. Maintainability	Modularity, reusability, analysability,
    modifiability and testability
    8. Flexibility	Adaptability, scalability and installability
    9. Safety	Operational constraints, risk identification, fail-safe
    behaviour and safe integration

    Source: ISO/IEC 25010:2023. ISO+1

    Note that the 2023 edition uses /Interaction capability/ instead of
    the previous /Usability/ characteristic and /Flexibility/ instead
    of /Portability/.


      3. Mapping ISO 25010 characteristics to architectural viewpoints

    This is where the two standards become particularly useful together.

    I would create a mapping between the nine quality characteristics
    and the viewpoints used in your architecture reviews.

    Here is a suggested starting point:

    ISO 25010 characteristic	Recommended viewpoints
    Functional suitability	Functional, Context, Process/Behaviour
    Performance efficiency	Performance, Deployment, Technology, Process/
    Behaviour
    Compatibility	Integration/Interface, Context, Technology
    Interaction capability	Functional, User Interaction, Operational
    Reliability	Operational, Deployment, Technology, Process/Behaviour
    Security	Security, Data, Integration, Deployment, Operational
    Maintainability	Logical, Application/Software, Development/Construction
    Flexibility	Logical, Application/Software, Technology, Deployment
    Safety	Safety, Functional, Operational, Deployment

    This is not a normative ISO mapping. It is a proposed mapping for a
    practical architecture review framework.

    There are two important observations:

    *1. One quality characteristic can require several viewpoints.*

    For example, reliability cannot be assessed simply by looking at the
    deployment architecture.

    You might need to examine:

      * Component dependencies.
      * Redundancy and failover.
      * Error handling.
      * Recovery mechanisms.
      * Monitoring and operational procedures.

    *2. One viewpoint can address several quality characteristics.*

    For example, the deployment viewpoint can contribute to assessments of:

      * Performance efficiency.
      * Reliability.
      * Security.
      * Flexibility.
      * Safety.

    This many-to-many relationship is perfectly consistent with the ISO
    42010 conceptual model.


      4. My recommendation: A combined architecture review framework

    If I were designing a repeatable architecture review process, I
    would organize it into three levels.


          Level 1: Architecture viewpoints

    Define a standard set of viewpoints that you use when examining
    architectures.

    For example:

    ID	Viewpoint
    VP01	Context
    VP02	Functional
    VP03	Logical
    VP04	Information/Data
    VP05	Application/Software
    VP06	Integration/Interface
    VP07	Technology/Infrastructure
    VP08	Deployment
    VP09	Security
    VP10	Operational
    VP11	Development/Construction
    VP12	Performance
    VP13	Safety

    This provides a consistent structure for your reviews.


          Level 2: Quality characteristics

    Use ISO 25010 to identify the quality characteristics relevant to
    the system.

    I would use all nine characteristics as an initial checklist, but
    not necessarily evaluate all nine in equal depth.

    For each characteristic, identify:

      * Relevant quality requirements.
      * Stakeholder expectations.
      * Architectural decisions that influence the characteristic.
      * Potential architectural risks.
      * Evidence needed to assess the characteristic.


          Level 3: Architecture assessment

    This is where you actually evaluate the architecture.

    For each relevant combination of viewpoint and quality
    characteristic, examine:

    Assessment dimension	Example questions
    Architectural adequacy	Does the architecture support the required
    quality?
    Completeness	Are all necessary architectural elements present?
    Consistency	Are architectural decisions consistent across views?
    Risks	What architectural weaknesses or risks exist?
    Trade-offs	What compromises have been made?
    Evidence	What documentation, models, tests or measurements support
    the assessment?

    For example:

    *Performance efficiency × Deployment viewpoint*

      * Is the system horizontally scalable?
      * Are there potential bottlenecks?
      * Is load balancing implemented appropriately?
      * Are resource limits understood?
      * Are there single points of failure?

    *Maintainability × Logical viewpoint*

      * Are components sufficiently modular?
      * Are responsibilities clearly separated?
      * Are dependencies manageable?
      * Can individual components be modified independently?
      * Is the architecture overly complex?

    *Security × Integration viewpoint*

      * Are interfaces properly protected?
      * Is authentication handled consistently?
      * Are trust boundaries clearly defined?
      * Is communication appropriately protected?
      * Are external dependencies considered?


      5. An additional recommendation: Include architectural principles

    I would also consider adding a third reference model: architectural
    principles and design principles.

    For example:

      * Separation of concerns.
      * Loose coupling.
      * High cohesion.
      * Encapsulation.
      * Information hiding.
      * Single responsibility.
      * Defense in depth.
      * Fail-safe defaults.
      * Least privilege.
      * Explicit interfaces.
      * Minimize dependencies.
      * Design for change.

    These principles help explain /why/ an architecture is good or
    problematic.

    You can then establish the following relationship:

    Element	Purpose	Example
    ISO 42010	How to examine the architecture	Deployment viewpoint
    ISO 25010	What quality to evaluate	Reliability
    Architectural principles	What design properties to look for	
    Eliminate single points of failure
    Architecture assessment	How well the architecture performs	
    Identified availability risks


      6. Suggested structure for your architecture review checklist

    I would ultimately build a review catalogue with the following
    structure:

     1. *Architecture viewpoint*
          * Context
          * Functional
          * Logical
          * Data
          * Integration
          * Deployment
          * Security
          * Operational
          * etc.
     2. *Quality characteristic*
          * Functional suitability
          * Performance efficiency
          * Compatibility
          * Interaction capability
          * Reliability
          * Security
          * Maintainability
          * Flexibility
          * Safety
     3. *Review criteria*
          * Specific questions to be answered.
     4. *Assessment*
          * Satisfactory
          * Partially satisfactory
          * Unsatisfactory
          * Not applicable
          * Not assessable (insufficient evidence)
     5. *Findings*
          * Identified strengths.
          * Weaknesses.
          * Risks.
          * Recommendations.
     6. *Evidence*
          * Architecture diagrams.
          * Design documents.
          * Configuration information.
          * Test results.
          * Measurements.


        7. Relevant standards

    I would use these as the primary references:

      * ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 — Architecture description and
        viewpoints. ISO 42010 official standard <https://www.iso.org/
        standard/74393.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com>
      * ISO/IEC 25010:2023 — Product quality characteristics. ISO 25010
        official standard <https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html?
        utm_source=chatgpt.com>
      * ISO/IEC 25019 — Quality-in-use model, a useful complement when
        evaluating the quality experienced by users.
    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        Final recommendation

    I would avoid creating two independent checklists, one based on ISO
    42010 and another based on ISO 25010.

    Instead, create a *unified architecture assessment matrix*.

    The central idea is:

    *Use ISO 42010 to organize the architecture examination and ISO
    25010 to organize the quality assessment.*

    This gives you:

      * A consistent approach to reviewing different systems and
        software architectures.
      * Coverage of the most important architectural concerns.
      * Traceability between quality requirements and architecture
        decisions.
      * A repeatable way to identify architectural weaknesses.
      * A foundation for comparing architectures across different projects.

    One particularly useful next step would be to develop a
    comprehensive architecture review catalogue containing perhaps 100–
    150 assessment questions, organized by viewpoint and mapped to ISO
    25010 characteristics. That would give you a reusable framework for
    conducting systematic architecture reviews.

 4.


    Yes. I think this is a very good idea. In fact, I would recommend
    using *ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 as an additional reference for defining
    the assessment dimensions and evaluation criteria*.

    It gives your architecture review methodology a stronger foundation
    because it provides established terminology and criteria for
    assessing requirements quality.

    There is, however, an important distinction:

      * *ISO 42010:* Defines how to describe and examine architectures
        using viewpoints, views, stakeholders and concerns.
      * *ISO 25010:* Defines the quality characteristics against which
        the system can be evaluated.
      * *ISO 29148:* Defines characteristics and quality criteria for
        requirements and requirements specifications.

    These standards address different aspects of the assessment and can
    complement each other.


        1. Which ISO 29148 characteristics can you reuse?

    ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 distinguishes between characteristics of
    individual requirements (Clause 5.2.5) and characteristics of a set
    of requirements (Clause 5.2.6).

    This distinction is particularly interesting for architecture reviews.

    The following characteristics are defined for individual requirements:

    ISO 29148 characteristic	Potential application to architecture reviews
    Necessary	Are the architectural elements and decisions actually needed?
    Appropriate	Is the level of architectural detail appropriate?
    Unambiguous	Are architectural descriptions and decisions clearly
    understandable?
    Complete	Does the architecture description provide all necessary
    information?
    Singular	Are architectural decisions and responsibilities
    sufficiently distinct?
    Feasible	Can the architecture realistically be implemented?
    Verifiable	Can architectural properties and constraints be
    objectively assessed?
    Correct	Does the architecture correctly reflect stakeholder
    requirements and constraints?
    Conforming	Does the architecture follow applicable standards,
    conventions and templates?

    For sets of requirements, ISO 29148 additionally addresses:

      * Completeness.
      * Consistency.
      * Feasibility.
      * Comprehensibility.
      * Ability to be validated.

    These are particularly interesting when assessing an architecture as
    a whole rather than individual architectural elements. Studylib+1


        2. My proposed assessment dimensions

    I would actually expand the assessment dimensions from my previous
    answer using ISO 29148 as inspiration.

    I would suggest the following catalogue:

    ID	Assessment dimension	Description
    AD01	Completeness	Does the architecture sufficiently address all
    relevant stakeholder concerns and requirements?
    AD02	Consistency	Are the architectural elements, decisions and views
    mutually consistent?
    AD03	Correctness	Does the architecture correctly address the
    intended system requirements and constraints?
    AD04	Feasibility	Can the architecture be implemented within
    technical, organizational and financial constraints?
    AD05	Appropriateness	Is the architectural solution appropriate for
    the system's context and level of complexity?
    AD06	Traceability	Can architectural decisions be traced to
    requirements, stakeholder concerns and constraints?
    AD07	Verifiability	Can architectural properties and claims be
    objectively verified?
    AD08	Modularity	Are architectural responsibilities and components
    appropriately separated?
    AD09	Understandability	Is the architecture understandable to its
    intended stakeholders?
    AD10	Extensibility	Can the architecture accommodate foreseeable changes?
    AD11	Compliance	Does the architecture comply with applicable
    standards, regulations and architectural principles?
    AD12	Risk management	Are architectural risks identified and
    adequately addressed?

    Some of these dimensions are directly inspired by ISO 29148, while
    others extend the approach into architecture-specific assessment.

    One important point: I would not apply the 29148 definition of
    completeness literally. For example, completeness of an individual
    requirement is different from completeness of an architecture
    description. You need to adapt the criteria to the object being
    assessed.


        3. How the three standards fit together

    I would structure your assessment methodology as follows:

    Standard	Role in the methodology	Main question
    ISO 42010	Architecture viewpoints	What aspects of the architecture
    should we examine?
    ISO 25010	Quality characteristics	What quality properties should the
    architecture support?
    ISO 29148	Assessment dimensions	What criteria should we use to
    evaluate the architecture?

    This creates a three-dimensional assessment model.

    For example:

    *Maintainability × Logical viewpoint × Completeness*

    Assessment questions:

      * Are all relevant software components represented?
      * Are their responsibilities documented?
      * Are dependencies identified?
      * Are important architectural constraints documented?

    Another example:

    *Reliability × Deployment viewpoint × Feasibility*

    Assessment questions:

      * Are redundancy mechanisms architecturally supported?
      * Can the proposed failover mechanisms be implemented with the
        available infrastructure?
      * Are the required availability targets technically achievable?
      * Are there infrastructure constraints that make the architecture
        infeasible?

    And another:

    *Security × Integration viewpoint × Consistency*

    Assessment questions:

      * Are security mechanisms consistently applied across interfaces?
      * Are authentication and authorization approaches consistent?
      * Are trust boundaries represented consistently in the architecture?


        4. One further suggestion: Separate assessment dimensions from
        quality characteristics

    I would resist adding every ISO 29148 characteristic directly to ISO
    25010.

    They serve different purposes.

    For instance:

      * Completeness is an assessment dimension.
      * Security is a quality characteristic.
      * Integration is a viewpoint.

    Keeping these concepts separate makes the methodology more reusable.

    You can apply completeness to security, maintainability,
    performance, reliability and other quality characteristics.

    Likewise, you can apply consistency to virtually every architectural
    viewpoint.

    This is a significant advantage of the three-dimensional model.

    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        5. My recommended final model

    I would use the following structure as the foundation for your
    architecture review framework:

    Dimension	Based on	Examples
    Viewpoints	ISO 42010	Logical, data, deployment, security, operational
    Quality characteristics	ISO 25010	Reliability, security,
    maintainability, flexibility
    Assessment dimensions	ISO 29148 + architecture-specific criteria	
    Completeness, consistency, feasibility, traceability, verifiability

    The resulting model is:

    *Architecture assessment = Viewpoint × Quality characteristic ×
    Assessment dimension*

    This gives you a systematic way to derive individual review questions.

    For reference, the relevant standards are:

      * ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 <https://www.iso.org/standard/74393.html?
        utm_source=chatgpt.com>
      * ISO/IEC 25010:2023 <https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html?
        utm_source=chatgpt.com>
      * ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 <https://www.iso.org/
        standard/72089.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com>

    *My strongest recommendation:* Build your review catalogue around
    these three dimensions rather than around a flat list of questions.
    This will make it much easier to maintain, extend and apply
    consistently to different kinds of systems and software architectures.
