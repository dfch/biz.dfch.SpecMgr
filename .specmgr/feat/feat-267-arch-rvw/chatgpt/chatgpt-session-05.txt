 2.


      Validation of the Applicability Matrix

    *Overall assessment: the matrix is a good foundation, and I would
    retain its basic structure. However, I recommend several corrections
    and clarifications before treating it as the baseline for an
    architecture assessment catalogue.*

    My assessment is that the matrix is conceptually sound, but it needs
    refinement in three areas:

     1. Mathematical consistency and matrix semantics.
     2. Completeness and balance of the viewpoint-to-quality mappings.
     3. Rules for applicability, tailoring and safety assessment.

    I would distinguish between two questions:

      * *Is the matrix internally consistent?* This can be checked
        objectively.
      * *Are the assignments architecturally appropriate?* These require
        expert judgement and should be validated against the intended
        assessment scope.


        1. Conformance with ISO/IEC 25010:2023

    The nine quality characteristics are correct, and their order
    matches ISO/IEC 25010:2023.

    Code	Quality characteristic	Assessment
    FS	Functional Suitability	Correct
    PE	Performance Efficiency	Correct
    CO	Compatibility	Correct
    IC	Interaction Capability	Correct
    RE	Reliability	Correct
    SE	Security	Correct
    MA	Maintainability	Correct
    FL	Flexibility	Correct
    SA	Safety	Correct

    The 2023 edition introduced several relevant changes compared with
    the 2011 edition:

      * Safety became a separate quality characteristic.
      * Usability was replaced by Interaction Capability.
      * Portability was replaced by Flexibility.
      * Scalability became a subcharacteristic of Flexibility.
      * Resistance became a subcharacteristic of Security.

    Your terminology reflects the 2023 model rather than accidentally
    mixing the 2011 terminology.

    One important distinction: ISO/IEC 25010 defines a *product quality
    model*, not an architecture viewpoint model. Your matrix is
    therefore a proposed architectural assessment framework that uses
    ISO quality characteristics as its quality dimension. That is
    perfectly reasonable, but it should be explicitly documented.

    Sources: ISO/IEC 25010:2023 — official ISO publication <https://
    www.iso.org/standard/78176.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com> and IEC
    publication record <https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/90024?
    utm_source=chatgpt.com>.


        2. Mathematical consistency: primary-cell count

    There is one definite error.

    Your stated count is *30 primary cells*, but the table contains *28*.

    Here is the breakdown:

    Viewpoint	Primary cells	Count
    VP01 Context	FS, CO	2
    VP02 Functional	FS, IC	2
    VP03 Logical	MA, FL	2
    VP04 Information/Data	CO, RE, SE	3
    VP05 Application/Software	MA, FL	2
    VP06 Integration/Interface	CO, RE, SE, FL	4
    VP07 Technology/Infrastructure	PE, FL	2
    VP08 Deployment	PE, RE, FL	3
    VP09 Security	SE	1
    VP10 Operational	PE, RE, MA	3
    VP11 Development/Construction	MA, FL	2
    VP12 Runtime/Process	PE, RE	2
    VP13 Safety	RE, SA	2
    *Total*		*28*

    Correction: VP06 contains four primary cells, not three. The total
    is therefore 28.

    *Recommendation:* Change the stated count from 30 to 28.

    This is worth correcting before the matrix becomes a reference for
    catalogue sizing or assessment coverage.


        3. Architectural assessment of the assignments

    The distribution is generally plausible. I would not attempt to make
    the matrix symmetrical: different viewpoints naturally have
    different relationships with quality characteristics.

    However, some assignments deserve particular attention.


          3.1 Functional Suitability (FS)

    Currently:

      * VP01 Context: P
      * VP02 Functional: P
      * VP03 Logical: S
      * VP04 Information/Data: S
      * VP05 Application/Software: S
      * VP09 Security: S
      * VP12 Runtime/Process: S
      * VP13 Safety: S

    *Assessment: Mostly good.*

    The two primary assignments make sense:

      * Context establishes what the system must achieve and its boundaries.
      * Functional describes the functions and responsibilities that
        fulfil those needs.

    I would retain these assignments.

    One consideration: logical and application architectures can also be
    important sources of functional suitability evidence. Keeping them
    secondary is reasonable if the assessment focuses on functional
    completeness and correctness in VP02, with architectural cross-
    checks elsewhere.


          3.2 Performance Efficiency (PE)

    Currently, primary in:

      * VP07 Technology/Infrastructure
      * VP08 Deployment
      * VP10 Operational
      * VP12 Runtime/Process

    *Assessment: Good.*

    This is a particularly coherent distribution.

    The four viewpoints cover different performance concerns:

    Viewpoint	Performance focus
    Technology/Infrastructure	Capacity and resource availability
    Deployment	Resource allocation and deployment topology
    Operational	Monitoring, capacity management and performance objectives
    Runtime/Process	Execution behaviour, latency and throughput

    I would retain all four primary assignments.

    One important consideration: Performance Efficiency also includes
    resource utilization and capacity. These should not become
    exclusively infrastructure concerns. Your secondary assignments in
    Logical, Information/Data, Application and Integration provide
    appropriate coverage.


          3.3 Compatibility (CO)

    Currently:

      * VP01 Context: P
      * VP04 Information/Data: P
      * VP06 Integration/Interface: P

    *Assessment: Good, with one potential omission.*

    Compatibility includes:

      * Co-existence.
      * Interoperability.

    VP06 is an obvious primary viewpoint.

    VP04 is also appropriate because information structures, semantics,
    formats and data exchange are central to interoperability.

    VP01 is useful because external systems, actors and environmental
    dependencies establish the compatibility context.

    I would consider whether VP05 Application/Software should be primary
    instead of secondary.

    My preference, however, would be to retain it as secondary:
    application-level compatibility can be assessed through integration
    contracts and technology choices without duplicating the integration
    assessment.


          3.4 Interaction Capability (IC)

    Currently:

      * VP02 Functional: P
      * VP01 Context: S
      * VP05 Application/Software: S
      * VP09 Security: S
      * VP10 Operational: S
      * VP13 Safety: S

    *Assessment: Reasonable, but the applicability rule needs refinement.*

    ISO/IEC 25010:2023 defines Interaction Capability in relation to
    interaction between specified users and a system through its user
    interface.

    This means that your emphasis on human interaction is appropriate.

    However, I would avoid the expression:

        For back-end systems without human users, mark the whole column
        inapplicable.

    There are back-end systems without ordinary end users but with:

      * Administrators.
      * Operators.
      * Support personnel.
      * Maintenance personnel.
      * Emergency operators.

    These people may interact with the system through management
    interfaces, monitoring consoles or administrative tools.

    *Suggested rule:*

    Apply Interaction Capability wherever the assessed product provides
    user-facing, operator-facing, administrative or maintenance
    interfaces. If no relevant human interaction exists within the
    assessment scope, mark the characteristic inapplicable with one
    scope-level justification.

    This is more precise and avoids overlooking operational interfaces.


          3.5 Reliability (RE)

    Currently primary in:

      * VP04 Information/Data
      * VP06 Integration/Interface
      * VP08 Deployment
      * VP10 Operational
      * VP12 Runtime/Process
      * VP13 Safety

    *Assessment: Very good.*

    Reliability is appropriately distributed across the architecture.

    The assignments cover:

      * Data integrity and persistence.
      * Integration failure handling.
      * Deployment resilience.
      * Operational availability and recovery.
      * Runtime fault tolerance.
      * Safety-related failure behaviour.

    I particularly like the primary assignment in VP13.

    Safety and reliability are closely related, but they are not
    interchangeable. A reliable system is not necessarily safe, and a
    safe system may require specific behaviour when reliability is
    compromised.

    The distinction should be preserved in the criteria catalogue.


          3.6 Security (SE)

    Currently:

      * VP04 Information/Data: P
      * VP06 Integration/Interface: P
      * VP09 Security: P

    *Assessment: Good.*

    The three primary assignments are defensible.

    They represent:

      * Information protection.
      * Communication and integration protection.
      * Cross-cutting security architecture.

    The Security viewpoint should remain the home of the comprehensive
    security criteria.

    The other primary cells should focus on their respective
    architectural concerns rather than reproducing the entire security
    assessment.

    I would also retain secondary security checks in Technology/
    Infrastructure, Deployment, Runtime and Operational.

    These are important places where security architecture decisions
    become concrete.


          3.7 Maintainability (MA)

    Currently primary in:

      * VP03 Logical
      * VP05 Application/Software
      * VP10 Operational
      * VP11 Development/Construction

    *Assessment: Good.*

    This distribution makes architectural sense.

    In particular:

      * Logical architecture determines structural modularity.
      * Application architecture determines software decomposition.
      * Development architecture determines construction practices.
      * Operational architecture determines maintainability in service.

    I would retain these assignments.


          3.8 Flexibility (FL)

    Currently primary in:

      * VP03 Logical
      * VP05 Application/Software
      * VP06 Integration/Interface
      * VP07 Technology/Infrastructure
      * VP08 Deployment
      * VP11 Development/Construction

    *Assessment: Good, although the scope needs careful definition.*

    Flexibility is broader than portability.

    Under ISO/IEC 25010:2023, it includes:

      * Adaptability.
      * Scalability.
      * Installability.
      * Replaceability.

    Your distribution is appropriate because these properties are
    influenced by architectural structure, technology, integration,
    deployment and development decisions.

    I would ensure that the catalogue explicitly covers scalability. It
    is particularly important not to reduce Flexibility to technology
    portability.


          3.9 Safety (SA)

    Currently:

      * VP13 Safety: P
      * All other applicable viewpoints: S, except VP11.

    *Assessment: Good conceptual decision.*

    I support having a dedicated Safety viewpoint and a dedicated Safety
    quality characteristic.

    The distinction between them is important:

      * *VP13 Safety* assesses the architectural mechanisms and
        decisions that establish and preserve safety.
      * *SA* assesses the quality of the product concerning safety.

    This creates a useful cross-check mechanism.

    One assignment I would reconsider is VP11 Development/Construction × SA.

    I would lean towards S rather than a dash.

    Development practices can affect safety through:

      * Safe coding practices.
      * Verification and validation.
      * Configuration control.
      * Safety-related change management.
      * Traceability of safety requirements.

    There is a legitimate argument for excluding these concerns from the
    architecture assessment itself, but I would not exclude them
    automatically.

    My recommendation: change VP11 × SA from |-| to |S|.

    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        4. Structural issues in the matrix

    Beyond individual assignments, I see three issues worth resolving
    before expanding the criteria catalogue.


          4.1 Define the difference between primary and secondary more
          precisely

    Your definitions are:

      * P: Full criteria.
      * S: A few cross-check criteria.

    This is understandable, but there is a potential ambiguity.

    For example, Security has three primary cells:

      * VP04 × SE
      * VP06 × SE
      * VP09 × SE

    If all three contain the full Security criteria, you will inevitably
    duplicate assessment work.

    I recommend defining P and S as follows:

    Marking	Recommended definition
    P	Primary assessment responsibility. Contains the complete set of
    criteria relevant to that viewpoint-characteristic intersection.
    S	Secondary assessment responsibility. Contains selected cross-check
    criteria that validate or complement primary assessments.
    -	No default assessment criteria. Can be activated through tailoring.

    The important distinction is that *P does not mean the entire
    quality characteristic is assessed independently in every primary cell.*

    It means full coverage of the relevant concerns within that viewpoint.

    This is particularly important for Security, Reliability and
    Flexibility.


          4.2 Distinguish applicability from assessment responsibility

    I would slightly change your terminology.

    A dash does not necessarily mean that a quality characteristic is
    inapplicable.

    It means that you have not assigned default assessment
    responsibility to that intersection.

    For example:

      * Security × Technology may be secondary or primary depending on
        the architecture.
      * Functional Suitability × Deployment may become relevant for
        certain deployment-related functional constraints.
      * Interaction Capability × Runtime may be relevant where runtime
        interaction behaviour is important.

    Therefore, I recommend using:

    |- = No default criteria assigned|

    rather than:

    |- = Not applicable by default|

    This avoids conflating two different concepts:

     1. Whether a quality characteristic applies to the assessed system.
     2. Whether a particular viewpoint has assessment responsibility for it.

    This distinction will make the tailoring rules considerably cleaner.


          4.3 Define what happens to secondary cells during tailoring

    You currently specify that removing a primary cell requires
    justification.

    But what happens if an assessor decides that a secondary cell is
    unnecessary?

    I suggest:

      * Removing P requires a written justification.
      * Removing S requires no justification, unless the assessment
        methodology explicitly requires that cross-check.
      * Activating |-| requires no justification, but the reason should
        be recorded if it introduces additional assessment scope.

    This keeps the assessment proportionate without weakening its
    accountability.

    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        5. Review of the tailoring rules

    I would retain your existing rules, with some refinements.

    Rule	Assessment	Recommendation
    Removing P requires justification	Good	Retain
    Activating a dash is allowed	Good	Retain
    IC mostly concerns user interfaces	Good	Clarify operators and
    administrators
    Safety is opt-in	Good	Retain, with explicit scope justification
    Counts and markings require review	Good	Retain


          Safety gate

    I particularly support the safety gate.

    It prevents two undesirable situations:

     1. Safety being silently ignored in a safety-relevant system.
     2. Safety assessment being unnecessarily imposed on systems where
        it is genuinely outside the scope.

    I would slightly strengthen the rule.

    When safety is declared relevant:

      * VP13 must be included.
      * The SA column must be included.
      * Primary and secondary criteria must be assessed according to the
        matrix.
      * Exclusions of individual primary cells require justification.

    When safety is declared not relevant:

      * VP13 is excluded.
      * The SA column is excluded.
      * The justification is recorded at assessment-scope level.

    A subsequent change in safety relevance requires a new documented
    decision.

    One additional consideration: safety relevance should not be
    determined solely by whether the system is directly involved in
    controlling physical equipment. Software that influences decisions
    or actions with potential safety consequences may also be safety-
    relevant.

    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        6. My recommended changes

    I would make only a small number of changes to your original matrix.

    Location	Current	Recommendation	Reason
    VP11 × SA	-	S	Development practices can influence safety
    IC tailoring rule	No human users	No relevant human interaction	
    Includes operators and administrators
    Dash definition	Not applicable by default	No default criteria
    assigned	Separates applicability from responsibility
    P definition	Full criteria	Full viewpoint-specific criteria	Avoids
    duplication
    S tailoring	Not specified	Define removal rules	Completes tailoring logic

    I would *not* make broad changes to the remaining P/S assignments at
    this stage.

    They are sufficiently defensible for an initial assessment framework.


        7. An additional recommendation: validate the matrix against
        representative architectures

    Before expanding the criteria catalogue, I would perform a small
    validation exercise.

    Use three contrasting architectural scenarios:

    Scenario	What it validates
    Traditional enterprise application	Functional, application, data,
    integration and operational coverage
    Distributed cloud-native platform	Performance, flexibility,
    reliability, deployment and security coverage
    Safety-relevant cyber-physical system	Safety gate, reliability/
    safety interaction and infrastructure coverage

    For each scenario, ask three questions:

     1. Does every important architectural quality concern have at least
        one primary assessment location?
     2. Are the secondary cells providing useful cross-checks rather
        than duplicating primary criteria?
     3. Are there important quality concerns that cannot be naturally
        assigned to any viewpoint?

    This is more valuable than trying to establish theoretically perfect
    P/S assignments without testing them against real architectures.

    ------------------------------------------------------------------------


        8. Final verdict

    My assessment:

    Dimension	Verdict
    ISO/IEC 25010:2023 alignment	Very good
    Viewpoint coverage	Good
    Primary/secondary distinction	Good, needs clarification
    Security coverage	Very good
    Reliability coverage	Very good
    Safety integration	Very good
    Interaction Capability applicability	Needs refinement
    Tailoring rules	Good, incomplete
    Readiness for catalogue development	Nearly ready

    *I would approve this matrix as a draft foundation, subject to the
    clarifications above.*

    The most important architectural decision I would preserve is the
    separation between:

      * Quality characteristics, which describe /what quality properties
        need to be assessed/.
      * Viewpoints, which describe /where those properties become
        architecturally visible/.
      * Primary and secondary cells, which describe /the assessment
        responsibility and depth/.

    That separation gives the methodology a strong foundation.


          One final architectural principle

    I would explicitly add this principle to the methodology:

        A quality characteristic may be relevant across multiple
        architectural viewpoints, but its assessment should be
        coordinated to avoid duplicated criteria, inconsistent
        conclusions and gaps in coverage. Primary cells establish
        assessment responsibility; secondary cells provide complementary
        evidence and cross-checks.

    This principle will become particularly important when you start
    developing the actual assessment criteria.

    *My recommendation: freeze the matrix structure after these
    refinements, then validate it against the three representative
    architectures before investing in the detailed criteria catalogue.*
